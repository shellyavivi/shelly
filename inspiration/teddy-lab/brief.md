# Teddy Lab — Logo Brief
_Intake shortened at the user's request: anything not answered is marked (assumed) or (open, agent decides)._

## Project
- **Brand:** Teddy Lab. A new brand.
- **What it is:** a machine for the build-a-bear experience that makes or assembles plush bears.
  (assumed) It sits in retail or experience spaces, and customers watch or take part as their bear is made.
- **Relationship to Build-A-Bear Workshop:** (open, agent decides) the mark should work on its own and
  sit comfortably next to a parent retail brand.
- **Deliverable:** logo exploration only.

## Messaging hierarchy
| Level | Content |
|---|---|
| 1. Core idea | (assumed) **Craft you can watch.** A modern machine that makes a personal plush with real care. |
| 2. Pillars (ranked) | 1. Process & craft · 2. A shared experience (people making something together; the person and the machine) · 3. Cute plushies · 4. Modern · 5. Style |
| 3. Personality | Fresh, modern, "handfelt" (hand-made warmth; literal felt, stitch and fabric cues are allowed), professional |
| Anti-personality | **Never generic "AI-generated" looking** (glossy gradients, over-rendered 3D mascots, cliché sparkle). **Never too detailed.** |
| 4. Audience | (assumed) Families with kids roughly 4–12, plus teens and adult plush collectors. Parents decide. |
| 5. Constraints | (assumed) Latin script, international. Must work on the machine's signage, on-screen UI, an app icon or favicon, and as an embroidered or woven label on the plush. Must survive one colour and small sizes. |

## Open (agent decides)
- What "modern" means here: (assumed) somewhere between a design-led toy brand (Lego, Miffy, Muji)
  and a clean lab or tool aesthetic (Teenage Engineering, Braun). Each direction can sit at a different point.
- Competitors: (assumed) Build-A-Bear's own identity, Jellycat, Squishmallows, claw-machine and kiosk graphics. Look different from all of them.
- Type of mark: open.
- Risk appetite: distinctive but safe for the final choice. The Concept direction can be unexpected but buildable.

## Search plan
- **Proven:** best-in-class marks for toy, kids and craft brands done with restraint: friendly rounded
  wordmarks, simple bear symbols, and stitch details used sparingly.
- **Stand Out:** break the cute-toy convention with lab or industrial precision. Think of a technical,
  engineered wordmark that is still warm, and a "lab" system on a grid.
- **Concept:** candidate territories are "the seam / the single stitch line", "pattern-cutting
  diagrams and sewing patterns", and "the bear as a product spec / blueprint".
- **Non-design references:** textile and sewing photography, soft-form architecture, art such as
  Claes Oldenburg's soft sculptures and Anni Albers' weaving. Avoid kitsch and anything over-rendered.
